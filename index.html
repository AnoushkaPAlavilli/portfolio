Hello! I am a third-year PhD student in the Robotics Institute at Carnegie Mellon University, in Pittsburgh, PA. I am co-advised by Professor Jeff Schneider and Professor Guanya Shi. My research is in reinforcement learning (RL) and optimal control for autonomous off-road driving. I am currently interested in sim-to-real challenges in this domain, including vision and deformable terrain.

Last summer, I interned at FieldAI, where I was advised by Shehryar Khattak, Jason Gibson, and Charles Noren. My research focused on learning physics-structured dynamics models for skid-steer mobile robots, with the longer-term goal of creating a cross-embodiment dynamics model. I have also interned at NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory for two summers, during which I did off-road driving research and created a machine learning prediction tool for Mars 2020 mission operations. Previously, I interned at MIT Lincoln Laboratory in the Advanced Sensors and Techniques Group in learning for signal and image processing.

In my undergraduate studies at Tufts University in Medford, MA, I had many incredible mentors, including my research advisors Professor Mai Vu and Professor Mike Levin.

    TinyMPC: Model-Predictive Control on Resource-Constrained Microcontrollers
    Anoushka Alavilli*, Khai Nguyen*, Sam Schoedel*, Brian Plancher, and Zachary Manchester
    In IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2024

    Best Paper Award in Automation Abs arXiv Code Website

    Winner of the Best Paper Award in Automation.

    Model-predictive control (MPC) is a powerful tool for controlling highly dynamic robotic systems subject to complex constraints. However, MPC is computationally demanding, and is often impractical to implement on small, resource-constrained robotic platforms. We present TinyMPC, a high-speed MPC solver with a low memory footprint targeting the microcontrollers common on small robots.
